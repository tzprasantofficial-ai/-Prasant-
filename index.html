01 — ABOUT
THE SHORT VERSION

I'm Prasant — raised in Shillong, built in the UAE. My road ran through airport gates with Bangkok Airways, call floors in Dubai and nine-plus years of face-to-face sales, and every stop taught me the same thing: people don't buy products, they buy stories. Today I tell those stories with video, motion graphics and design — directing AI tools like a small production crew to turn any brief into finished, on-brand work.

Off the clock you'll find me heat-pressing shirts at U-Design, building QR tech for Al Ain's shops, teaching UAE businesses AI through Aakriti — or on final approach in a virtual A320. Fluent in English, Hindi, Nepali and Urdu. Powered by curiosity and cutting-edge AI.

08 — MY LIFE · MY PEOPLE

Fourteen Cats & Counting

None of them were planned. Every one of them is family. I'm the guy who eats chicken yet can't meet the eyes of a goat in a transport truck without whispering sorry — compassion isn't logical, it's inherited. My family have been givers and animal people since before I could walk. Here's how a small studio in Al Ain became a full house, chapter by chapter.

CAT PHOTO 01 — COCO
Chapter 01 — Where it began

Coco

New studio, living alone, thinking about getting a pet — then I found a small cat inside my neighbour's place and "rescued" her, sure she'd wandered in by mistake. Named her Coco after serious research into the best cat names. Her spay surgery later terrified me — I was scared of losing her — but we both came out stronger, and life restarted.

CAT PHOTO 02 — MOMMY & THE QUEENS
Chapter 02 — The rainy season

Mommy & the Four Queens

A pregnant stray I'd been feeding outside needed shelter when the rains began. The plan was simple: keep her safe until the babies came. She had four girls — Rani, Princess, Angel and Queen — no name research needed this time, and "Mommy" just stuck.

CAT PHOTO 03 — THE SECOND LITTER
Chapter 03 — Fate's turn

The Second Litter

Mommy slipped away — and came back with four more kittens. Three girls and one little boy. I tried everything I could, but he couldn't be saved. He's the reason I've never said no to a rescue since. Nine cats now shared one small roof — and I learned to walk like a dancer, small steps only, because one big step could hurt somebody.

CAT PHOTO 04 — LALA
Chapter 04 — The parking lot

Lala

A kitten dropped near my workplace parking, with injuries. I fed her thinking surely someone would take her home. That someone, in the end, was me.

CAT PHOTO 05 — DULLI
Chapter 05 — Lightning twice

Dulli

A few months later — same parking lot, same story. Meet Dulli. Apparently that parking lot knows my weakness.

CAT PHOTO 06 — RAJA
Chapter 06 — The window

Raja

A fearless, vocal outdoor kitten — his shy sister always waiting behind — made a habit of sitting on my window ledge, exactly where my indoor cats sleep. The glass is one-way; he couldn't see in. On one side: 24-hour food, water and AC. A pane apart: a cat fighting the desert heat. I started letting him in. Then he vanished for a week, his sister too. When I finally found him, alone, the decision made itself. Raja — the king.

CAT PHOTO 07 — THE FIGHTER
Chapter 07 — The long way

The Fighter

Meows in the dark outside my door. Next morning I found her by the flower pot — broken leg, lower body not working. Surgery would cost my entire month's salary with no promise of a cure. So we did it the long way: a little house in my washroom with her own food, water and washroom corner, cleaning her every day, walking practice, medicines, play. Three months to stay alive. A year later, she runs like all the others.

CAT PHOTO 08 — PHILIPPINE & KABAHAN
Chapter 08 — The outdoor family

Philippine & Kabahan

Someone left a mother and four very, very cute kittens outside my building. The house was full, so I built them a cardboard home with cushions, food and water. Then one kitten went missing. Three days later I found another gone forever. Decision made: the mom and her last two — Philippine and Kabahan — moved in.

CAT PHOTO 09 — THE LAST RESCUE
Chapter 09 — Two months ago

The Last Rescue

Both mother cats eventually left for lives of their own — one came back pregnant, then got lost. Of her litter I could save just one, home with me for two months now. I'm still searching for her mom. The door stays open.
